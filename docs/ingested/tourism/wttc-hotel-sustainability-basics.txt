Source: https://wttc.org/initiatives/hotel-sustainability-basics
Publisher: World Travel & Tourism Council (WTTC)

Hotel Sustainability Basics is a globally recognised and coordinated set of sustainability indicators that all hotels should implement as a minimum. Developed by the industry for the industry, they represent the 12 actions that are fundamental to hotel sustainability. WTTC is calling on destinations and accommodation stakeholders around the world to implement the criteria and raise the bar of sustainability in the sector.

The Hotel Sustainability Basics are intended as the first step of a progressive sustainability journey that can then be expanded to follow more ambitious pathways, such as Pathway to Net Positive Hospitality or the GSTC standards. Tracking and benchmarking progress help hotels understand environmental and social impacts and identify operational enhancements. Resource efficiency and smarter supply chain management can lead to cost savings. Basic sustainability indicators can help organisations take first steps towards evolving regulatory landscapes and mandatory reporting requirements.

Meet the Basics:

Efficiency: measure and reduce energy use; measure and reduce water use; identify and reduce waste; measure and reduce carbon emissions; clean coils and filters; LED lights; digital thermostats; water fixtures; landscaping.

Planet: linen reuse programme; green cleaning products; vegetarian options; no plastic straws or stirrers; no single-use plastic water bottles; bulk amenity dispensers; clean coils and filters; LED lights; digital thermostats; water fixtures; landscaping.

People: community benefit; reduce inequalities; accessibility; staff training; anti-modern slavery; promote local culture.

Verification timeline: Year 1 — 8 criteria; Year 2 — 9 or more criteria; Year 3 — 12 criteria; Year 4 — advance to greater sustainability. The initiative says the 12 criteria cover both back-of-house and guest-facing activities and include environmental and social aspects.
